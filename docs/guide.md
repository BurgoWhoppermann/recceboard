# User guide

## The board

The board is an open canvas. Shots sit where you put them, and the **reading
order is the arrangement** — left to right, top to bottom, the way a page reads.
Move a shot and you have changed the cut.

- **Tap** a card to select it. Selected, it shows a **⋯** button for its actions.
- **Press and hold**, then drag, to move it. It glows when it's ready to move.
- **Drag one card onto another** to swap their places.
- **Press and hold empty canvas** to add something exactly there.
- **Pinch** to zoom, drag to pan.
- **Double-tap** a shot for its actions, or a note to start typing.

Shots placed close together on the same line are joined by a dotted line: they
read as one **scene**. Pull one away and the link breaks. Reordering works inside
a scene, so rearranging two shots doesn't disturb the rest of the board.

### Selecting several

Tap the rectangle-select tool, then drag a box around what you want. Drag any one
of them to move the lot; delete them together from the banner at the top. On a
Mac there's no tool to arm — dragging on empty canvas always draws the box.

## Recording

The camera opens already framed to the board's format. Tap the picture to focus
and meter there; slide up or down from that spot to correct the exposure; hold to
lock focus and exposure. The lens buttons are labelled in millimetres — the real
35mm-equivalent focal length of each camera, so it means the same thing on any
iPhone. Turned upright, they fall back to magnification, because a focal length
describes a landscape frame.

Stop recording and you land in the trim. Set the in and out points, mute the shot
if its sound isn't wanted, then **Done** — or **Next**, which keeps it and returns
to the camera for the next one.

A new shot is named after the lens it was taken on. It's an ordinary name; edit
it or clear it.

## Importing

**⋯ → Extras**, or the **+** button: from Photos or from Files, several at once.
Each one goes through the same preparation as a recording — clips through the
trim, stills through crop and duration — one after another until the batch is
done. A clip in the wrong shape gets a reframe, so you choose what to keep rather
than having the middle taken.

## Sound

Each shot keeps its own sound unless you mute it. On top of that:

- A **voice-over for a shot**, recorded from that shot's own screen.
- A **voice-over for the whole board**, recorded while the film plays.
- A **music bed** under everything.

Levels for all three sit in the Audio panel and can be moved at any time —
they're mixed live, so changing one costs nothing.

## Playing and exporting

**Play** runs the board as a film. Tap the middle to pause, the sides to step a
cut back or forward, or scrub the timeline. It loops.

**Export** gives you the film as an MP4, the board as a PDF contact sheet, every
shot as its own video, or one still per shot. Frame lines, if set, are burned in.

## Working with someone else

**⋯ → Collaborate** turns a board into a shared one and gives you a code of
six characters, shown as something like K7R-M92. Anyone entering it under
**Join with a Code** can open the board and add to it. The code expires after a
week.

The owner can see who has access and remove people, or stop sharing altogether.
Someone who was invited can leave; leaving takes the board off their device and
leaves it with everyone else.

## Settings

**⋯ → Settings**, and they apply to this device, not to one board:

- **Recording quality**, 360p to 4K. Applies to shots added from then on.
- **Appearance**, to hold the app in light or dark whatever the system does.
- **Snap to grid**.
- **Rebuild previews**, if a looping preview came out wrong.

## Keyboard

With a hardware keyboard, on iPad or Mac:

| | |
|---|---|
| ⌘N | New board |
| ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z | Undo / redo |
| ⌘C / ⌘V | Copy / paste a shot |
| ⌘D | Duplicate a shot |
